# 02 — Step-by-Step Learning Path

Complete each stage in order. A stage is complete only when the stated artefact can be explained to a sceptical executive, auditor, or regulator.

## Stage 1 — Establish the enterprise context

**Objective:** Understand what the organisation does, which services are critical, and where security and compliance obligations attach.

**Study**

- Business model, products, legal entities, locations, and material outsourced services.
- Critical business services, dependencies, data classification, and resilience requirements.
- Three lines model: business and technology ownership, independent risk and compliance challenge, and internal audit assurance.

**Do**

1. Draw a service-to-asset-to-control map for one critical service.
2. Identify five assumptions that could make the map incomplete.
3. Define who can accept each category of residual risk.

**Evidence:** service map, stakeholder map, and assumptions log.

## Stage 2 — Build the obligation and control framework

**Objective:** Convert external expectations into owned, testable, and evidenced controls.

**Study**

- Applicability and obligations management.
- Control objectives versus control activities.
- Common control mapping and avoiding duplicate testing.
- Policy hierarchy: principles, standards, procedures, and records.

**Do**

1. Select one obligation and write its plain-language intent.
2. Map it to a control objective, control activity, owner, frequency, and evidence.
3. Define the test procedure and what would constitute a failure.

**Evidence:** obligation-to-control traceability matrix and control test script.

## Stage 3 — Run assurance and manage observations

**Objective:** Lead an audit or examination with disciplined evidence and issue governance.

**Study**

- Risk-based assurance planning and independence.
- Evidence request design, sampling, interviews, walkthroughs, and evidence quality.
- Finding validation, severity, root cause, action planning, and closure testing.

**Do**

1. Build an assurance plan for one quarter.
2. Write one observation using condition, criteria, cause, consequence, and recommendation.
3. Challenge a weak action plan until it addresses the root cause.

**Evidence:** assurance plan, evidence index, issue record, and closure memo.

## Stage 4 — Operate regulatory and PCI-DSS readiness

**Objective:** Maintain readiness without creating a document-only compliance programme.

**Study**

- Regulatory request intake, ownership, response quality, and commitment tracking.
- Cardholder data environment (CDE) identification, scope validation, segmentation assumptions, and evidence.
- Vulnerability, access, logging, change, incident response, and service-provider dependencies.

**Do**

1. Create a mock regulatory request tracker with response approvals.
2. Define the CDE boundary and list scope assumptions that must be validated.
3. Compare a policy statement with operational evidence and record the gap.

**Evidence:** request tracker, CDE scope statement, evidence index, and readiness heatmap.

## Stage 5 — Make risk visible to senior management

**Objective:** Give decision-makers a concise and honest view of cyber risk and compliance.

**Study**

- Risk appetite, thresholds, KRIs, KCIs, trend analysis, and aggregation.
- Risk acceptance and escalation.
- Committee narrative: what changed, why it matters, what is being done, and what decision is needed.

**Do**

1. Reduce a 20-row issue register to five executive messages.
2. Separate facts, assumptions, decisions, and recommendations.
3. Present one red item without using unexplained technical jargon.

**Evidence:** committee pack, speaking notes, and decision log.

## Stage 6 — Build and lead the GRC function

**Objective:** Design a sustainable function that scales beyond individual heroics.

**Study**

- Mandate, operating model, roles, skills, quality assurance, and succession.
- Annual planning, capacity allocation, tooling, metrics, and continuous improvement.
- Constructive challenge: independent enough to be credible, practical enough to be useful.

**Do**

1. Design a 12-month roadmap with outcomes, dependencies, and measures.
2. Define role profiles for regulatory compliance, assurance, risk, and policy governance.
3. Establish a monthly operating rhythm and quality review.

**Evidence:** GRC charter, roadmap, RACI, hiring plan, and operating calendar.

## Role-readiness review

Ask a peer to score a recorded 10-minute briefing:

| Dimension | Ready when you can… |
|---|---|
| Judgement | State the risk, uncertainty, options, recommendation, and escalation path. |
| Evidence | Explain why evidence is sufficient, current, attributable, and reproducible. |
| Ownership | Name one accountable owner and distinguish support from accountability. |
| Independence | Challenge management without taking operational ownership away from the first line. |
| Executive presence | Lead with impact and decision required, then provide detail on request. |
| Regulatory credibility | Avoid over-claiming, preserve traceability, and meet commitments. |
| Leadership | Set standards, coach the team, and improve the system rather than relying on heroics. |
