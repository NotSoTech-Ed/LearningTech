# 03 — Scenario-Based Learning

## How to practise

For each case, take 15 minutes to prepare and 5 minutes to brief. Use the answer guide only after recording your first response. A strong answer identifies facts, unknowns, risk, owner, immediate containment, sustainable treatment, evidence, decision rights, and communication.

## Scenario 1 — Supervisory request with incomplete evidence

### Situation

A supervisor requests an enterprise view of privileged access reviews, exceptions, and overdue remediation within five business days. Technology says the review is complete, but two business units use spreadsheets and one critical vendor has not supplied evidence.

### Your task

Prepare the response approach and a two-minute update to the CISO.

### Expected decisions

- Who owns the response and who approves it?
- What can be stated as fact versus still being validated?
- What immediate risk reduction is required?
- How will the vendor and spreadsheet evidence be handled?

### Answer guide

Create a single request record with scope, interpretation, accountable owners, evidence status, quality review, approvals, and submission date. Do not present “in progress” as “complete.” Validate the population, sampling basis, review frequency, exceptions, and evidence attribution. Escalate the missing vendor evidence and use a clearly labelled interim statement if permitted. Assign a dated remediation plan and retain the audit trail.

## Scenario 2 — Audit observation that keeps recurring

### Situation

Internal audit finds that quarterly access recertification is late for the third consecutive quarter. Management proposes a reminder email and a new dashboard. The actual cause is unclear ownership after reorganisations.

### Your task

Challenge the action plan and recommend closure criteria.

### Answer guide

The reminder and dashboard address symptoms. The root cause may include unclear accountable ownership, incomplete joiner/mover/leaver data, incompatible review tooling, or no escalation threshold. The sustainable plan should define service ownership, population completeness, review workflow, evidence retention, escalation, and effectiveness testing across multiple cycles. Closure requires independent validation that the control operated effectively—not merely that the dashboard exists.

## Scenario 3 — PCI-DSS scope dispute

### Situation

A product team says its payment service is “out of PCI scope” because card data is tokenised by a provider. Network diagrams show an integration path from the product environment to the provider, and the provider's responsibility matrix is six months old.

### Your task

Lead a scope validation workshop.

### Answer guide

Do not accept a scope assertion without evidence. Establish data flows, trust boundaries, systems that can affect payment security, administrative access, logging, segmentation controls, shared responsibility, and provider attestations. Document assumptions and test segmentation if it is relied upon. Refresh the responsibility matrix and record residual gaps. Engage the qualified assessor and relevant legal/compliance stakeholders where interpretation is material.

## Scenario 4 — Critical observation with a missed deadline

### Situation

A high-severity encryption gap is due for closure in 10 days. The permanent solution is delayed by a platform migration. Operations proposes accepting the risk informally until migration completes.

### Your task

Prepare the escalation and recommendation for the risk committee.

### Answer guide

Escalate before the due date with impact, affected assets/data, exposure period, dependencies, interim controls, residual risk, and decision required. A compensating control must be specific, operating, evidenced, and tested. Formal risk acceptance requires the authorised owner, defined expiry, conditions, and monitoring. The migration is not itself a risk treatment unless it demonstrably reduces the risk. Track the permanent fix as a committed action with independent closure testing.

## Scenario 5 — Executive committee reporting

### Situation

The committee pack contains 47 open issues, 12 overdue actions, three incidents, and 18 policy exceptions. Members say they cannot tell whether risk is improving.

### Your task

Replace the pack with five decision-oriented messages.

### Answer guide

Aggregate by material service and risk theme rather than counting everything equally. Show trend, ageing, risk appetite threshold, top accountable owners, and decisions required. Separate incidents from control weaknesses, and exceptions from accepted risk. Include data quality caveats. Every red item should have an owner, next milestone, target date, and escalation consequence.

## Scenario 6 — Building the GRC function

### Situation

The organisation has capable people but no common control taxonomy, inconsistent evidence, duplicated audits, and no reliable view of remediation capacity.

### Your task

Propose a 90-day stabilisation plan and a 12-month target state.

### Answer guide

First stabilise: agree mandate and decision rights, inventory obligations and critical services, define minimum issue and evidence standards, baseline open commitments, and establish weekly risk triage. Then standardise: create a common control library, map ownership, rationalise assurance, introduce quality review, and build executive metrics. Finally scale: implement tooling where it removes manual risk, develop capability, measure sustainable closure, and review the operating model quarterly.

## Scoring rubric

Score each scenario from 0 to 3:

| Score | Description |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Describes activity but misses ownership, evidence, or risk. |
| 1 | Identifies the issue and a plausible action, but lacks governance or sustainability. |
| 2 | Covers risk, ownership, evidence, escalation, and closure with minor gaps. |
| 3 | Gives a concise, defensible, decision-ready response and anticipates challenge. |

Target **15/18** overall, with no score below 2 in ownership, evidence, or escalation.
