# 01 — Preparation and Baseline

## 1. Role interpretation

The role is not only a compliance coordinator. It is an enterprise leadership role that connects regulatory expectations, cyber risk, control design, operational evidence, and senior decision-making.

The target profile combines 14+ years of relevant Information Security GRC, cyber risk, regulatory compliance, and audit experience with strong executive and stakeholder-management capability. CISA, CISSP, and/or CISM should be treated as a preparation track as well as a credential requirement: connect each certification domain to an example you can explain from practice.

| Role signal | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| Regulatory and supervisory engagement | Maintain a reliable regulatory inventory, coordinate responses, control commitments, and communicate clearly with supervisors. |
| Audits and assurance | Build an annual assurance plan based on risk, scope, evidence quality, and independence. |
| Closure of observations | Own the lifecycle from finding validation to root cause, action plan, testing, acceptance, and sustainable closure. |
| Enterprise frameworks | Harmonise policies and controls across products, technology, operations, vendors, and locations. |
| PCI-DSS | Protect the cardholder data environment, maintain scope discipline, and produce defensible evidence of control operation. |
| Executive reporting | Convert technical and control data into decisions, trends, risk acceptance, and accountability. |
| GRC leadership | Build capability, operating rhythm, quality standards, and trusted relationships across the enterprise. |

## 3. Preparation pack

Create a private practice pack using fictional or sanitised information only:

1. Organisation profile: products, legal entities, locations, critical services, and data types.
2. Stakeholder map: board or committee, CISO, CIO, risk, legal, compliance, internal audit, operations, product, procurement, and key vendors.
3. Obligation register: source, requirement, applicability, owner, mapped control, evidence, review date, and status.
4. Asset and service view: critical business services, crown-jewel data, dependencies, and recovery requirements.
5. Control library: control objective, risk addressed, owner, frequency, evidence, test procedure, and known gaps.
6. Issue register: observation, severity, root cause, action, accountable executive, due date, dependency, residual risk, and closure evidence.
7. Reporting baseline: open issues, overdue actions, incidents, exceptions, risk acceptance, assurance coverage, and key trends.

## 4. Core vocabulary

- **Design effectiveness:** whether a control, if performed as designed, would address the risk.
- **Operating effectiveness:** whether the control operated consistently and produced reliable evidence during the review period.
- **Observation:** a control or compliance weakness supported by evidence; do not label a condition a finding before validating facts and criteria.
- **Root cause:** the systemic reason the condition occurred, not the immediate symptom.
- **Compensating control:** an alternate control that reduces the same risk when the primary control cannot operate as intended.
- **Residual risk:** risk remaining after controls and planned treatments.
- **Risk acceptance:** an explicit decision by an authorised owner to retain residual risk within a defined period and threshold.
- **Regulatory readiness:** the ongoing ability to demonstrate governance, control operation, evidence integrity, and accountable decision-making—not a last-minute document collection exercise.

## 5. First-week build exercise

Using the templates in [`templates.md`](templates.md), produce:

- A one-page GRC charter.
- A 20-row obligation register.
- A 15-row issue register containing at least three overdue items.
- A one-page committee dashboard.
- A 30-minute stakeholder interview plan.

Do not use real customer, employee, credential, incident, or regulator-confidential data.
