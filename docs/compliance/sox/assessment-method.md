# SOX ITGC Assessment Method

## 1. Assessment objective

Assess whether in-scope ITGCs are suitably designed and operated effectively for the defined financial reporting period, and whether control exceptions are evaluated and remediated in accordance with management and auditor requirements.

## 2. Roles and independence

| Role | Accountability |
|---|---|
| Audit committee / board | Oversight of ICFR reporting, significant deficiencies, material weaknesses, and remediation. |
| CFO / controller | Owns financial reporting assertions, management conclusion, and business control response. |
| CIO / technology executive | Owns technology environment and resources for remediation. |
| SOX programme owner | Coordinates scope, risk-control matrix, testing, evidence, issues, and reporting. |
| Control owner | Performs the control and retains complete evidence. |
| Control reviewer | Reviews operation and exceptions with appropriate independence. |
| Internal audit / compliance | Provides independent challenge or testing as defined in the assurance plan. |
| External auditor | Performs audit procedures under its professional standards and engagement scope. |
| Service organisation owner | Evaluates SOC 1 coverage, complementary controls, and exceptions. |

## 3. Phase 1 — Plan and scope

Create a scoping memo containing:

- Reporting entity, fiscal year, and locations.
- Material accounts, disclosures, assertions, and significant processes.
- In-scope applications, interfaces, reports, infrastructure, and providers.
- Key ITGCs and application controls relied upon.
- System-generated reports and end-user computing population.
- Prior-year findings, changes, acquisitions, migrations, and new services.
- Reliance on service auditor reports and complementary user entity controls.
- Thresholds, materiality approach, and escalation contacts.

## 4. Phase 2 — Walkthrough and design evaluation

For each key control, trace one item from initiation through completion and evidence retention. Confirm:

1. The stated risk is relevant to an assertion.
2. The control is precise enough to detect the risk.
3. The performer has the right competence and authority.
4. The review is sufficiently independent and evidenced.
5. The population is complete and the control covers the full period.
6. Exceptions are investigated, corrected, and escalated.
7. Dependencies are included in scope.

Classify design as `Effective`, `Needs improvement`, or `Ineffective`. Do not proceed to operating effectiveness testing for a control whose design cannot address the stated risk without first recording the design deficiency.

## 5. Phase 3 — Operating effectiveness testing

Test controls across the entire period, not only near year end. For recurring controls, select a representative sample across periods, owners, systems, locations, and high-change events. Document:

- Population definition, completeness, and source.
- Sample method and rationale.
- Items selected and replacements.
- Evidence date, performer, reviewer, and approval.
- Exceptions, re-performance, and management explanation.
- Conclusion and impact on reliance.

For automated controls, test the configuration, relevant general controls, logic, interfaces, and evidence that the automated process ran successfully.

For reports, test parameters, source data completeness, logic, access, change control, and reconciliation to an independent source.

## 6. Phase 4 — Deficiency evaluation

For every exception:

1. Validate the fact and period.
2. Identify affected control, assertion, account, system, and population.
3. Determine whether the exception is isolated or systemic.
4. Assess compensating controls and their precision.
5. Evaluate likelihood and magnitude with finance and controllership.
6. Aggregate with related deficiencies.
7. Determine disclosure, management letter, or audit committee escalation.
8. Define remediation and retest criteria.

Do not label a control failure immaterial solely because no misstatement was found. The assessment concerns the reasonable possibility of a misstatement and timely prevention or detection.

## 7. Phase 5 — Remediation and retest

A remediation is sustainable only when the root cause is addressed and the revised control operates for an appropriate period with reliable evidence. Retesting must cover:

- Revised design and implementation.
- Population completeness.
- Operating performance across multiple instances or periods.
- Exception handling and escalation.
- Dependencies and downstream reporting.
- Any residual risk accepted by authorised management.

Evidence created solely for retest does not prove prior-period operation.

## 8. Assessment conclusion

The conclusion should reconcile:

- Controls planned, tested, passed, failed, and not tested.
- Deficiencies by severity and affected financial reporting areas.
- Compensating controls and remaining exposure.
- Open remediation and target dates.
- Changes from prior year.
- Management's ICFR conclusion and auditor communications.

## 9. Suggested annual banking cadence

| Period | Activity | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Scope, risk assessment, prior-year follow-up, system change review | Approved scoping memo and risk-control matrix |
| Q2 | Walkthroughs, design testing, provider report review | Design conclusions and evidence plan |
| Q3 | Interim operating effectiveness testing | Exceptions, early remediation, interim conclusion |
| Q4 | Year-end testing, roll-forward, deficiency aggregation | Management conclusion and audit committee pack |
| Post year-end | Retest, lessons learned, control rationalisation | Remediation closure and next-cycle improvements |
