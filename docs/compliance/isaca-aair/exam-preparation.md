# AAIR Exam Preparation

## 1. Exam-aligned study allocation

Use the official current content outline as the source of truth. The current outline describes a 90-question exam across:

- **Domain 1 — AI Risk Governance and Framework Integration (37%)**
- **Domain 2 — AI Life Cycle Risk Management (21%)**
- **Domain 3 — AI Risk Program Management (42%)**

Study time should broadly follow the weighting, while ensuring every domain is understood. Domain 3 deserves the largest practice allocation because it covers scenario identification and assessment, treatment, controls management, metrics, supply chain, incident response, business impact analysis, business continuity, and disaster recovery.

## 2. Question-solving method

For each question:

1. Identify the decision, affected party, and risk impact.
2. Identify whether the question is testing governance, life cycle, or programme management.
3. Look for the most proportionate and risk-based action.
4. Prefer accountability, independent challenge, evidence, and monitoring over a vague policy or a tool purchase.
5. Eliminate answers that ignore scope, human oversight, legal/privacy impact, or residual risk.
6. Mark uncertainty and move on; return during review.

## 3. Original practice questions

These are created for this repository and are not ISACA exam questions.

### Question 1

A bank plans to use a generative AI assistant to draft customer-service responses. It can access internal procedures and selected customer records. What should happen first?

A. Buy a prompt-filtering product.  
B. Approve the model because a human sends the final response.  
C. Define the use case, data access, affected customers, risk tier, ownership, and prohibited outputs.  
D. Require the vendor to provide a generic security certification.

**Answer: C.** Governance and impact precede tool selection. Human review may reduce risk but does not remove privacy, leakage, accuracy, or accountability concerns.

### Question 2

A model's accuracy remains stable, but the customer population changes materially and outcomes for one segment worsen. What is the best response?

A. Continue operation because the accuracy metric is stable.  
B. Trigger reassessment using segment performance, impact, data drift, and fairness thresholds.  
C. Delete the model immediately without preserving evidence.  
D. Change the dashboard colour and wait for the annual review.

**Answer: B.** Risk monitoring must detect changes in context and affected outcomes, not only aggregate accuracy.

### Question 3

A foundation-model provider changes its model version without notice. Which programme capability is most directly required?

A. A documented change-impact process linked to provider governance, validation, and approval.  
B. A larger annual awareness course.  
C. A longer password maximum age.  
D. A new office visitor log.

**Answer: A.** Provider change can alter behaviour, risk, performance, and commitments; it requires notice, impact assessment, validation, and decision rights.

### Question 4

A high-impact AI decision produces an unsafe output during an incident. Which response is strongest?

A. Preserve logs, invoke the AI incident playbook, contain or suspend safely, assess affected parties, notify according to obligations, and investigate root cause.  
B. Delete the prompt and output to prevent reputational damage.  
C. Blame the vendor and wait for its report.  
D. Continue operation to preserve availability.

**Answer: A.** Incident response balances containment, evidence, impact, notification, recovery, and learning.

### Question 5

Which evidence best supports that an AI control operated effectively?

A. A policy approved two years ago.  
B. A vendor marketing document.  
C. Dated risk reviews, validation results, monitoring records, exception handling, and accountable approvals for the assessed period.  
D. A screenshot of a model dashboard with no population or timestamp.

**Answer: C.** Evidence must be attributable, period-appropriate, complete, and linked to the control and population.

## 4. Mock-exam protocol

Create an original 90-question mixed-domain set using the official domain weights as a guide. Use a timer and no reference material. Do not treat the percentage as a guaranteed passing threshold.

After the attempt, categorise every miss:

- Knowledge gap
- Misread question
- Wrong risk priority
- Governance versus technical confusion
- Weak life-cycle reasoning
- Weak incident/resilience reasoning
- Time management

Retake only after reviewing the reasoning, then maintain a fresh mixed-domain set. Readiness requires repeated consistency, not one lucky score.

## 5. Final readiness gate

Before scheduling, the learner should be able to:

- Explain all three domains without notes.
- Complete a full AI use-case assessment from intake through retirement.
- Defend a risk treatment and residual-risk recommendation to an executive committee.
- Design metrics, monitoring, incident response, BIA, continuity, and DR for a high-impact use case.
- Explain why an answer is proportionate, accountable, evidence-based, and aligned to risk.
- Confirm current ISACA eligibility, exam format, policies, and registration directly from official sources.
